A following list grows quietly. You follow people back to be polite, follow a conference hashtag’s speakers for a week, follow brands for a giveaway — and a few years later your feed is full of accounts you don’t recognise. Cleaning it up isn’t about hitting a ratio. It’s about making the list match what you actually want to read.

Here’s a process that works for lists of a few hundred or many thousands, and that you can repeat every few months without starting from scratch.

## Step 1: Decide what your feed is for

Write one sentence before touching anything. “Industry news and people I’ve worked with.” “Friends, football and local updates.” “Design inspiration.” That sentence is the test every account has to pass, and it stops cleanup turning into a numbers game.

If you run a brand or professional account, the sentence is usually about relevance to your audience: accounts you engage with, partners, and sources you share.

## Step 2: Get the full picture

You can’t clean what you can’t see. A scan gives you the whole list with useful context. With [UnfollowX Pro](/features), press **Scan my following** on x.com and open the dashboard. The Following page splits your list into:

- **Don’t follow back** and **Mutuals**
- **Not blue** — accounts without X Premium’s blue check
- **Inactive** — sorted by how long since each account last posted
- **All** and **Kept** (everything your rules protect)

## Step 3: Protect before you prune

Make the keep decisions first, while you’re thinking clearly about who matters. Add friends, colleagues, clients and must-read sources to your whitelist, and set Keep rules for whole groups — for example, keep verified accounts, keep anyone with “studio” or your company name in their bio, or keep accounts you followed in the last 30 days so new connections have time to follow back. The [whitelist guide](/blog/how-to-whitelist-accounts-before-unfollowing-on-x) has more examples.

## Step 4: Review in batches, easiest first

Large lists feel overwhelming because every account looks like a separate decision. Batching turns hundreds of decisions into a few:

1. **Clearly dormant accounts.** Inactive for a year or more. Most can go. See [finding inactive accounts](/blog/how-to-find-inactive-accounts-on-x).
2. **Likely spam or abandoned profiles.** Filter for no profile photo plus very few posts, or accounts that follow ten times more people than follow them.
3. **Non-followers you don’t recognise.** Search and skim — if a name means nothing and the bio doesn’t match your sentence from Step 1, it’s a candidate.
4. **Everything else**, at your own pace, maybe over several sessions.

UnfollowX Pro lets you save any selection as a **named group**, so you can build “Batch 2 — spam-looking” today and act on it tomorrow.

## Step 5: Use more than one signal

No single filter tells you whether an account belongs in your feed:

| Signal | What it suggests | What it doesn’t prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doesn’t follow back | The relationship is one-way | That the posts aren’t worth reading |
| Inactive for months | You’re not missing anything right now | That they’ve left X for good |
| No blue check | They don’t subscribe to X Premium | Anything about quality |
| Very few followers | A small or new account | That it’s spam |
| Follows far more than follow it | Possible follow-for-follow behaviour | Bad intent on its own |

When two or three signals line up — inactive, no photo, doesn’t follow back, not someone you recognise — the decision is usually easy.

## Step 6: Act at a measured pace

Select a batch and unfollow it with sensible pacing: random gaps between actions, breaks, and a daily limit. UnfollowX Pro’s **Balanced** preset uses 8–20 seconds between actions, a 6-minute break every 50 and 400 per day; **Safe** is slower. X applies its own limits to every account, so if X asks you to slow down or verify your account, stop and let it rest. The [mass unfollow guide](/blog/how-to-mass-unfollow-accounts-on-x) covers pacing in detail.

## Step 7: Keep a record and make it a habit

History keeps every account you unfollowed, so a mistake is a two-click fix with **Follow again** ([details here](/blog/how-to-recover-from-an-accidental-unfollow-on-x)). You can export history to CSV if you like a paper trail.

After the first big cleanup, a quick review every couple of months keeps things tidy: rescan, look at what’s new on the Inactive and Don’t follow back tabs, and remove a small batch.

## Frequently asked questions

### What’s a good following-to-followers ratio?

There isn’t a universal target. Ratios matter mainly at scale: once you follow 5,000 accounts, X applies [ratio-based follow limits](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-follow-limit). Below that, follow the accounts that make your feed useful. More context in [X following vs followers](/blog/x-following-vs-followers-explained).

### Should I unfollow everyone who doesn’t follow me back?

Only if your feed is meant to be purely social. Many of the most useful accounts — news, experts, creators — will never follow you back.

### How long does a big cleanup take?

Reviewing is the slow part. Unfollowing itself is limited by your daily limit, so 1,000 removals at 400 per day takes about three days of background running.

## The bottom line

A good cleanup starts with a purpose, protects the people who matter, and works through the rest in batches using several signals at once. Do it slowly the first time; after that, a short review every few months keeps your following list working for you.
