An inactive account is one that has stopped posting. Following a lot of them doesn’t hurt you, but it does make your following list a poor picture of who you actually read — and it inflates the number of accounts X counts against your follow limits. X has no built-in filter for inactive accounts, so finding them means checking when each account last posted.

## What “inactive” can — and can’t — mean on X

X doesn’t show when someone last logged in, scrolled or liked something. The only public signal is **activity you can see**: the date of an account’s most recent post, reply or repost. That’s what any honest inactivity check is based on.

It has limits worth keeping in mind:

- Someone can read X every day without posting. To an activity check, they look inactive.
- Private accounts hide their posts from people they haven’t approved, so their activity can’t be read.
- Pinned posts can be years old and aren’t a sign of recent activity, so a good check ignores them.

Treat “inactive” as *hasn’t posted in a while*, not *has left X*.

## Method 1: Check profiles manually

Open a profile and look at the date of the newest post that isn’t pinned, plus the Replies tab for accounts that mostly reply. It’s accurate and fine for a handful of accounts you’re curious about. For a list of hundreds, it’s impractical.

## Method 2: Use the Inactive tab in UnfollowX Pro

UnfollowX Pro reads each account’s newest readable post for you and sorts your following list by how long it’s been quiet.

1. [Install the extension](/setup), open x.com in Chrome signed in to your account, and press **Scan my following**.
2. Open the dashboard and go to **Following → Inactive**.
3. In the **Last-active check** box, choose which accounts to check — for example, only accounts that don’t follow you back — and start the check. It reads one profile at a time in the background and is read-only, so you can close the tab and come back.
4. Filter by **30d+**, **90d+**, **180d+** or **1y+**, and sort by **Most inactive first** or **Least inactive first**.

Each account shows “Last active: N days ago” with the date. Results are saved, so the same account isn’t read twice within a week. If X rate-limits the check, it pauses and continues by itself.

> [!NOTE] Accounts whose date can’t be read reliably — private or restricted posts, an unavailable account, or X not answering — are marked **Unavailable** with the reason and listed separately. They are not counted as inactive and can’t be selected. Accounts with no posts at all appear there as “Never posted”. **Retry unavailable** tries them again later.

## Choosing your cut-off

There’s no correct number of days; it depends on why you follow people.

| Cut-off | Good for |
| --- | --- |
| 30 days+ | Fast-moving topics where you expect regular posts |
| 90 days+ | A sensible default for most cleanups |
| 180 days+ | Keeping occasional posters, removing the clearly dormant |
| 1 year+ | Very conservative — accounts that have almost certainly stopped |

Combining inactivity with other filters makes the list sharper. For example: inactive for 180 days **and** doesn’t follow you back, or inactive **and** fewer than 10 posts in total.

## Accounts worth keeping even when quiet

Before you select anything, look for quiet accounts that still earn their place:

- **Occasional but valuable posters** — researchers, writers or friends who post a few times a year.
- **Official accounts** that only post during events, outages or announcements.
- **People you know**, whatever they post. A quiet friend is still a friend.

Add these to your whitelist (one tap on the shield) or use a Keep rule such as “keep verified accounts” or a keyword match. The [whitelist guide](/blog/how-to-whitelist-accounts-before-unfollowing-on-x) shows how.

## Removing inactive accounts

Select the accounts you want to remove on the Inactive tab and choose **Unfollow selected**. The run uses your usual pacing — random delays, breaks and a daily limit — and skips anyone protected by your whitelist or Keep rules. If you have a lot to remove, the [mass unfollow guide](/blog/how-to-mass-unfollow-accounts-on-x) explains how to choose a pace and what X’s limits mean.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does X show when someone was last online?

No. X doesn’t expose login or browsing activity. Inactivity checks rely on the date of the newest visible post, reply or repost.

### Why are some accounts “Unavailable” instead of inactive?

Because their activity couldn’t be read reliably — for example, the account is private, restricted or temporarily unavailable. Guessing would put active people on your removal list, so they’re kept separate.

### Is it worth unfollowing inactive accounts?

If you want your following list to reflect what you actually read, yes. It also frees room if you’re near X’s 5,000-follow threshold, where X applies [ratio-based follow limits](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-follow-limit).

## The bottom line

Inactivity on X can only be measured by visible posts, so use it as a review signal rather than a verdict. Pick a cut-off that matches your interests, protect the quiet accounts you value, and remove the rest at a steady pace.
