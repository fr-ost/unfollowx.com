Muting, unfollowing and blocking all reduce what you see from an account on X, but they do very different things to the relationship. **Unfollow** removes their posts from your Following timeline. **Mute** keeps the follow but hides their posts and notifications from you. **Block** cuts the connection both ways and stops them interacting with you. Picking the right one avoids awkwardness — and stops you unfollowing people you'd rather just quieten.

> [!TIP] **Quick answer:** want less of someone without them noticing? **Mute.** Don't want their posts in your feed at all? **Unfollow.** Want them to stop interacting with you? **Block.** Want them out of your followers without a block? Use **Remove this follower** on the web.

## The differences at a glance

| | Unfollow | Mute | Block |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| You still follow them | No | Yes | No (blocking also unfollows) |
| Their posts in your Following timeline | No | No | No |
| Their replies and mentions in your notifications | Yes, if they mention you | No | No |
| They can follow you | Yes | Yes | No |
| They can reply to, repost or like your posts | Yes | Yes | No |
| They are notified | No | No | No, but they may notice on your profile |
| Easy to undo quietly | Yes (follow again) | Yes (unmute) | Unblocking doesn’t restore follows |

X has changed details of blocking and visibility over time, so check X’s help pages on [muting](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-mute) and [blocking](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/blocking-and-unblocking-accounts) for the current behaviour.

## Unfollow: stop following, nothing more

Unfollowing simply ends your follow. Their posts stop arriving in your **Following** timeline, though X’s **For you** timeline can still recommend posts from accounts you don’t follow. X doesn’t notify anyone when you unfollow them, and nothing changes on their side: they can still follow you, see your public posts and reply.

Use unfollow when an account no longer belongs in your feed — it posts about things you’ve lost interest in, it’s inactive, or it was a follow-back you never really wanted. It’s also the only option that changes your following count, which matters if you’re near X’s [5,000-follow threshold](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-follow-limit).

Unfollowing a large number of accounts is where a tool helps; see [how to mass unfollow on X](/blog/how-to-mass-unfollow-accounts-on-x).

## Mute: keep the follow, lose the noise

Muting hides an account from your timelines and notifications while you keep following it. The muted account isn’t told, and from their side nothing looks different — you still appear in their followers. You can still see their posts by visiting their profile.

Mute is the polite choice for:

- **Friends or colleagues** whose posting has become too much, when an unfollow would be noticed or misunderstood.
- **Temporary noise** — someone live-posting an event you don’t care about. Mute, then unmute later.
- **Accounts you follow for a reason other than reading them**, such as a client or a community account.

X also lets you **mute words and phrases** rather than accounts, which is often better for topics you’re tired of.

## Block: end the connection

Blocking is the strongest option. A blocked account can’t follow you, and blocking removes any follow between you in both directions. Blocked accounts can’t message you or interact with your posts. They aren’t notified, but they may see that they’re blocked if they visit your profile.

Use block for harassment, spam or anyone you don’t want interacting with you. Unblocking later doesn’t restore the follows the block removed.

## Removing a follower without blocking

If the problem is that someone **follows you** — not that you follow them — you don’t need to block. On the web, X offers **Remove this follower** in the ⋯ menu on their profile. They stop following you and aren’t notified. Unless your account is private, they can follow you again.

Some people use a “soft block” (block, then immediately unblock) for the same effect; the remove-follower option is cleaner where it’s available.

## Choosing the right tool

| Your situation | Best option |
| --- | --- |
| A friend posts too much | Mute |
| An account went quiet years ago | Unfollow |
| You followed back to be polite and never read it | Unfollow |
| Someone is harassing you | Block (and report if needed) |
| A follower you don’t want, without drama | Remove this follower |
| A topic, not a person, is the problem | Mute words |
| You follow hundreds of accounts you don’t read | Bulk unfollow after a review |

## Frequently asked questions

### Does X notify someone when I mute or unfollow them?

No. X doesn’t send notifications for mutes or unfollows. A muted account still sees you as a follower.

### Can a muted account still see my posts?

Yes. Muting only changes what *you* see. If your account is public, they can still see and reply to your posts — you just won’t see their replies in your notifications.

### If I block someone, do they unfollow me?

Yes. Blocking removes the follow in both directions, and unblocking doesn’t restore it.

### Is it better to mute or unfollow?

Mute when you want to keep the connection but not the posts; unfollow when the account no longer belongs in your feed. If you’re cleaning up dozens or hundreds of accounts, unfollowing after a review is usually what you want.

## The bottom line

Unfollow to change your feed, mute to quieten someone without changing the relationship, block to stop someone interacting with you, and remove a follower when the issue is who follows you.
