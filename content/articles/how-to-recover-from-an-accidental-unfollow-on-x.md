It happens to everyone: a mis-tap on the **Following** button, or a bulk cleanup that caught someone you meant to keep. The fix is usually simple — follow the account again — but how you find them, and what happens next, depends on how the unfollow happened.

## If you unfollowed someone by hand

1. Go to their profile. If you don’t remember the handle, search their name or find one of their recent posts.
2. Press **Follow**.
3. If the account is private, X sends a new follow request that they’ll need to approve.

That’s it. If you can’t remember who it was, X won’t help you here — it doesn’t keep a list of accounts you unfollowed.

## If a tool unfollowed them during a cleanup

A good tool keeps a history so mistakes are easy to reverse. In [UnfollowX Pro](/features):

1. Open the dashboard and go to **History**.
2. On the **Unfollowed** tab, search for the name or handle. Each entry shows when it happened.
3. Select the account — or several — and press **Follow again**.

Follow-again actions use the same pacing, breaks and daily limit as unfollows, so re-following many people after a big mistake happens gradually rather than all at once. If X asks the extension to slow down or asks you to verify your account, it waits.

> [!TIP] Stopped a run because you spotted a mistake? Press **Stop** (or **Alt + Shift + S**) first, fix your whitelist, then re-follow from History. Nothing else is unfollowed while the run is stopped.

## What the other person sees

X doesn’t send notifications for unfollows, so many people never notice a brief accidental unfollow. When you follow again, the usual new-follower notification applies, depending on their notification settings. If you’re worried about how it looks, a short message usually settles it.

## What re-following does and doesn’t restore

Following again restores the follow relationship. It doesn’t undo anything else:

- **Private accounts** need to approve a new request.
- **Lists, mutes and notifications** you set for that account may need checking.
- **If they’ve blocked you** or changed their privacy settings in the meantime, X may not allow the follow.

## Prevent it next time

The best accidental-unfollow fix is the one you never need:

- **Whitelist people before any bulk run.** Whitelisted accounts are never unfollowed, in any mode. See [how to whitelist accounts before unfollowing](/blog/how-to-whitelist-accounts-before-unfollowing-on-x).
- **Use Keep rules** for groups — verified accounts, private accounts, keyword matches, or a grace period for accounts you followed recently.
- **Check the Kept tab** before starting, so you know who is protected.
- **Start small.** Unfollow one batch, review History, then continue.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I see a list of everyone I’ve unfollowed on X?

Not in X itself. UnfollowX Pro’s History lists every account the extension unfollowed or followed, with timestamps and CSV export. Unfollows you did by hand outside the extension aren’t included.

### Is there a time limit for re-following?

No. You can follow again whenever you like, subject to X’s normal follow limits and the account’s privacy settings.

### Will re-following lots of people cause problems?

Re-following counts as following, and X applies [follow limits](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-follow-limit) — including a technical limit of 400 follows per day. Re-follow at a measured pace.

## The bottom line

An accidental unfollow is almost always recoverable: find the account, follow again, and check privacy settings if it was a private account. If a tool did the unfollowing, use its history to find everyone at once — and whitelist the important people before your next cleanup.
