An X follower tracker shows how your followers change over time: who’s new, who left, and who follows you without being followed back. Every tracker — free or paid, app or extension — works by saving your followers list and comparing it with a later one. Understanding that one idea tells you what a tracker can measure, what it can’t, and how to read its results without jumping to conclusions.

## How a follower tracker works

1. **Read** your complete followers list.
2. **Save** it as a snapshot with the date.
3. **Read again** later and save a second snapshot.
4. **Compare**: accounts that appear only in the newer snapshot are *new followers*; accounts that appear only in the older one are *lost followers*.

Because X doesn’t keep a history of who unfollowed you, nothing can be compared until that first snapshot exists. The first check is always a baseline.

## What a tracker can tell you

- **New followers since your last check**, so you can follow back the people you want.
- **Lost followers since your last check**, with the window between the two checks.
- **Followers you don’t follow back** — the mirror image of a non-follower list.
- **Growth over time**, if the tracker keeps a history of totals.

## What a tracker can’t tell you

- **Why someone left.** A tracker reports a change, never the reason.
- **The exact moment** of an unfollow — only that it happened between two checks.
- **Anything before your first snapshot.** No tool can reconstruct past unfollows.
- **Whether a “lost” follower actually chose to unfollow.** Suspended, deactivated and deleted accounts drop out of follower lists too, and so does anyone who blocks you.

> [!NOTE] If a tracker compares an incomplete followers list as if it were complete, people who never left appear as “unfollowed”. Good trackers only compare complete reads. UnfollowX Pro keeps partial reads separate and skips the comparison when X returns an incomplete list, so nobody is listed by mistake.

## Reading results without overreacting

A short list of lost followers is normal for almost any account. Before acting on it:

- **Look for patterns, not individuals.** A spike after a particular post tells you more than any single name.
- **Check the profile** if a specific change matters. An unavailable profile suggests suspension or deactivation rather than a choice.
- **Don’t check too often.** Daily checks don’t reveal more changes; they just spread the same changes across more snapshots.

## What to do with tracker results

- **Follow back** new followers you want to connect with — select them on the New followers tab.
- **Unfollow** accounts that left, if you prefer a mutual list. Accounts you still follow are marked so you can select them.
- **Export** changes to CSV for your own records.

Follows and unfollows use the same pacing, breaks and daily limit, and X’s own [follow limits](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-follow-limit) still apply.

## Where the data lives

UnfollowX Pro stores follower snapshots and change records in Chrome extension storage on your computer. They aren’t uploaded to the developer. Optional anonymous usage statistics are separate, never include your account or lists, and can be turned off in Settings — the [privacy policy](/privacy) has the exact details.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a free X follower tracker?

Yes. UnfollowX Pro’s follower tracking is free, like every other feature, with no subscription or premium tier.

### Can a tracker show who viewed my profile?

No. X doesn’t make profile views available, and trackers only compare follower lists.

### Why did my follower count drop but the tracker shows no unfollowers?

Counts can update before a tracker’s next complete read, and X sometimes removes suspended or spam accounts in bulk. Run a new check and compare again.

## The bottom line

A follower tracker is a comparison tool, not a mind reader. Start tracking early, compare complete snapshots only, and treat each “lost follower” as a change worth a glance rather than a verdict.
