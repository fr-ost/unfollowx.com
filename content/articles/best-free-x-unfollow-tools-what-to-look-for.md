Search for a free X unfollow tool and you’ll find dozens of extensions, apps and web services with similar names and similar promises. They differ a lot once you look closely: what “free” covers, how they reach your account, whether they protect accounts you value, and what they do with your data. This guide is a checklist you can apply to any of them — including ours.

> [!NOTE] We make UnfollowX Pro, so we’re not neutral. That’s why this guide doesn’t rank competitors or quote their prices, which change often. Apply the checklist yourself and read each tool’s current listing and privacy policy.

## 1. What does “free” actually include?

“Free” covers very different models:

- **Free forever** — every feature is free with no paid tier.
- **Freemium** — a free plan with caps (for example, a small number of unfollows per day) and a paid plan for the rest.
- **Free trial** — full features for a limited time, then payment.
- **Free but ad- or data-supported** — the product is paid for some other way.

What to check: the store listing’s pricing details, the words “Pro”, “Premium” or “upgrade” inside the product, and whether key features — whitelist, CSV export, unfollower tracking — sit behind a paywall.

## 2. How does it access your X account?

There are three common approaches:

- **Your existing browser session** — an extension works inside the X tab you’re already signed in to. No password changes hands.
- **Sign in with X (OAuth)** — you authorise an app to act on your account through X’s official login. Check what permissions it asks for.
- **Your username and password** — a tool asks you to type your X credentials into its own page.

The first two are normal. Be very cautious with the third: you’re giving a third party your login. Whatever the method, you should be able to revoke access — by removing the extension, or from X’s connected apps settings.

## 3. Does it protect accounts you want to keep?

Bulk tools act on lists, and lists catch people you care about. Look for:

- **A whitelist** that is respected in every mode, not just some.
- **Rules for groups** — verified, private, big accounts, keywords, recent follows.
- **A way to review** the selection before anything happens.

## 4. Does it pace actions — and is it honest about X’s limits?

X applies its own limits to every account: its help center describes a [technical limit of 400 follows per day](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-follow-limit), ratio-based limits after 5,000 follows, and [rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation) that restrict automated and aggressive behaviour. A responsible tool:

- Spaces actions out with delays, breaks and a daily limit you can change.
- Backs off when X asks it to slow down and stops when X asks you to verify.
- Lets you pause, resume and stop at any time.
- **Doesn’t promise** that your account can’t be limited. No tool can guarantee that.

Be wary of “unlimited unfollows per hour” or “guaranteed safe” claims. “Unlimited” can honestly describe what a *tool* allows; it can’t override what *X* allows.

## 5. Can you undo mistakes?

A history of every action, with a way to follow accounts again, turns a mistake into a two-click fix. Without one, you’re relying on memory.

## 6. What happens to your data?

Read the privacy policy and the Chrome Web Store’s privacy section. Useful questions:

- Are your following list, followers and history stored locally or uploaded to a server?
- Does the tool collect analytics, and can you turn them off?
- Does it request broad permissions, such as access to all websites, that its features don’t need?

## 7. Is it maintained?

X changes its website regularly, and unmaintained tools break. Check the listing’s last-updated date, the changelog if there is one, and whether recent reviews describe it working on X’s current site.

## A one-page checklist

| Question | Good sign | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What does free include? | All features, no caps | Key features need an upgrade |
| How does it access X? | Existing session or official sign-in | Asks for your password |
| Protects important accounts? | Whitelist in every mode, rules | No whitelist |
| Pacing? | Delays, breaks, daily limit | “Unlimited per hour” |
| Honest about X limits? | Explains them | “Guaranteed safe” |
| Undo? | Action history, follow again | No history |
| Data? | Stored locally, clear policy | Vague or no policy |
| Maintained? | Recent updates | Last updated long ago |
