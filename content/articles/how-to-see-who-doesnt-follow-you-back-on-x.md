X doesn’t have a “not following you back” list. To see who doesn’t follow you back, you have three options: check the **Follows you** label on each profile, compare your following and follower lists yourself, or use a tool that does the comparison for you. Which one makes sense depends mostly on how many accounts you follow.

> [!TIP] **Quick answer:** install a non-follower checker such as [UnfollowX Pro](/twitter-non-followers-checker) (free), open x.com in Chrome, click the extension and press **Scan my following**. In about a minute you get a count and a reviewable list of every account you follow that doesn’t follow you back. The scan only reads — it doesn’t unfollow anyone.

## Method 1: Check the “Follows you” label by hand

When someone follows you, X shows a small **Follows you** label next to their name on their profile. No label means they don’t follow you (or X couldn’t show the relationship, which is rare).

1. Open your profile and select **Following**.
2. Open a profile from the list and look for **Follows you** beside the handle.
3. Repeat for each account.

This needs no tools and no permissions, and it’s perfectly reasonable if you follow fewer than a hundred or so accounts. Beyond that it gets slow, easy to lose your place in, and hard to repeat next month.

## Method 2: Compare your lists from X’s data archive

X lets you [download an archive of your account data](https://help.x.com/en/managing-your-account/accessing-your-x-data) from **Settings and privacy → Your account → Download an archive of your data**. The archive includes your following and follower lists as data files — typically account IDs with profile links rather than friendly names.

If you are comfortable with spreadsheets, you can put both lists side by side and find IDs that appear in “following” but not in “followers”. It’s accurate and fully under your control, with two catches: the archive can take a few days to prepare, and it’s a snapshot, so you’d repeat the whole process to check again later.

## Method 3: Use a non-follower checker

A non-follower checker reads both sides of the relationship and does the comparison instantly. With UnfollowX Pro, which runs as a Chrome extension inside the X session you already use:

1. Add [UnfollowX Pro from the Chrome Web Store](/setup). There is no account to create.
2. Open x.com in Chrome and make sure you’re signed in to the account you want to check.
3. Click the UnfollowX Pro icon in Chrome’s toolbar and press **Scan my following**.
4. Wait for the scan to finish. The popup shows three numbers: **Following**, **Don’t follow back** and **Mutuals**.
5. Choose **Review & pick** to open the dashboard. The **Following** page opens on the **Don’t follow back** tab.

The scan reads your list through the same Following timeline X’s website uses, including X’s own “follows you” flag for each account. It works in the background, so you can use other tabs while it runs, and it never asks for your password.

## How to read the results

A non-follower list is a starting point for review, not a verdict. A few things worth knowing before you act on it:

- **Big accounts rarely follow back.** News outlets, brands, public figures and specialist accounts you follow for their posts are often worth keeping regardless.
- **Inactive accounts show up too.** An account that stopped using X years ago won’t follow anyone back. The dashboard’s **Inactive** tab can show when each account last posted — see [how to find inactive accounts on X](/blog/how-to-find-inactive-accounts-on-x).
- **Lists change.** Someone may follow you back tomorrow. If a decision matters, rescan first.

In the dashboard you can search by name, handle or bio and filter by details such as no profile photo, very few posts, very few followers, or accounts that follow far more people than follow them. Sorting and filters are the fastest way to turn a list of several hundred non-followers into a short list you actually care about.

| Account type | Typical decision |
| --- | --- |
| Friend or colleague who doesn’t follow back | Usually keep — add to your whitelist |
| News, brand or creator you read | Keep if the posts are useful to you |
| Account that hasn’t posted in a year | Often safe to remove |
| Spammy or follow-for-follow account | Usually remove |
| Account you followed last week | Give it time — use a grace-period Keep rule |

## If you decide to unfollow non-followers

When you’re ready, protect the accounts you want to keep first. In UnfollowX Pro, a tap on the shield adds an account to your whitelist, and whitelisted accounts are never unfollowed in any mode. Keep rules can also protect verified accounts, private accounts, big accounts, keyword matches and recent follows. The full walkthrough is in [how to whitelist accounts before unfollowing](/blog/how-to-whitelist-accounts-before-unfollowing-on-x).

Then either press **Unfollow non-followers** in the popup, or select specific accounts in the dashboard and choose **Unfollow selected**. Runs are paced with random delays, breaks and a daily limit you control. X still applies its own limits to every account — the [mass unfollow guide](/blog/how-to-mass-unfollow-accounts-on-x) explains how to choose a sensible pace.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does X show who doesn’t follow you back?

Not as a list. X only shows the **Follows you** label on individual profiles, so finding every non-follower means checking profiles one by one or comparing lists.

### Will people know I checked?

No. Checking reads data your account can already see. UnfollowX Pro keeps scan results in Chrome extension storage on your computer, and X doesn’t notify anyone when you view your own lists.

### Do I need to give a tool my X password?

You shouldn’t. UnfollowX Pro works with the X session already signed in to Chrome and never asks for your password. Treat any tool that wants your password with caution.

### How often should I check?

Whenever you plan a cleanup. Every scan is a fresh read, so there’s no benefit in scanning daily unless your list is changing quickly.

## The bottom line

For a small list, the **Follows you** label is enough. For anything larger, a checker that compares the lists for you saves hours and makes the check easy to repeat. Either way, treat “doesn’t follow back” as one signal among several, protect the accounts that matter, and only then decide who goes. If you also want to know who *stopped* following you, read [how to find out who unfollowed you on X](/blog/how-to-find-out-who-unfollowed-you-on-x).
